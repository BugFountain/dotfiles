# Interactivity

Read this before a page keeps state, takes comments, or talks to Claude. [`walkthrough/runtime/walkthrough.js`](../walkthrough/runtime/walkthrough.js) implements everything below, and a review page gets it by rendering through `render.mjs`. A hand-painted page gets the same reading grammar from [`page.js`](../walkthrough/runtime/page.js) ([Hand-painted pages](#hand-painted-pages)). A page should be fun to walk: every action answers at once, on screen and, when sound is on, by ear.

## Static first

- The page prerenders every chapter, stop and hunk as HTML. JavaScript adds navigation, marks, the file view, comments, scenes and sound. Without it the page still reads from top to bottom.
- Off claude.ai (a local file or the live preview), every feature that needs no capability works in this browser alone. Progress lives in `localStorage` under `wt:<key>:`, where the key hashes the repo and the head SHAs, so two reviews never share state.
- On claude.ai, the page asks for each capability with `window.claude.use(name)`. A capability that is missing or refused hides its feature, never breaks the page.
- **One shared layer.** Comments are what a team shares on the page. Review marks, checked questions and the reader's place stay with each reader, so the page shows no presence, progress board or presenter.

## Navigation

The reader moves; the page follows. This is how Kit Langton's visual-effect pages work: the current item changes only on an explicit act, and the page scrolls to it.

- **The current stop changes only on an act:** `j` or `k` (or `←` and `→`, `↑` and `↓`), a click on the rail, a press inside a stop, a link, the address. Scrolling never moves it, never moves focus, and never plays a sound. There is no scroll-spy.
- **Keys land where the reader is.** `k` or `→` steps forward and `j` or `←` back, in the order the keys sit. While the current stop is on screen, they step from it. Once the reader scrolls it away, they step from the reading line under the header. `j` inside a long stop first returns to that stop's top. A key pressed while the last move still scrolls steps from the stop that move chose, so fast presses never bounce, and the page scrolls on its own spring: a new target bends the motion instead of restarting it.
- **`↓` and `↑` read.** They step the way effect.institute steps its sections, with one difference: a stop can be taller than the screen. While the stop being read runs on past the bottom edge, `↓` scrolls on 80% of a screen, and the press that finds the next stop's head within a fifth of a screen moves to it like `k`. `↑` scrolls back to the stop's head the same way, then moves to the previous stop. The opening and the close read by section the same way: the head, then each heading (`what changes`, `the whole flow`, `the layers`, `every question`), and past the opening's last section the first chapter opens. The file view reads its files the same way. An open sheet keeps the arrows for its own scrolling, and paging inside a stop or a section makes no sound, because the reader stays in the same place.
- **Focus follows the act.** A key move scrolls the stop, or in the file view the file, to the top and focuses it, so the Tab key and a screen reader start there. A section the arrows land on takes focus the same way: the whole opening or close at its head, the heading of any other section, and the flow itself on the flow's section, so the arrows step its hops at once and Tab goes on to its first control. A chapter that opens takes focus when focus sat in the chapter that closed, so the next Tab starts in the new one. The address records the stop, the view, the file and the hop on the opening's stage, so a link or a reload opens the same place. A page opened with no address keeps none, so the browser focuses nothing on load and the first Tab reaches the skip link.
- **The navigator** sits bottom right, outside the reading column: `home` for the page's top, `j ←`, where the reader is (`#5983 · 04/30`) over a tick per item of the level they move through, `k →`, `end` for the page's bottom, `z` for the open PR's fold, and `x reviewed`. In a PR the ticks are its stops, reviewed ones filled and the current one ringed, a branch stop smaller; on the opening and the close they are the stack's PRs, filled once reviewed. A tick is a pointer's shortcut, outside the Tab order, and names its stop on hover; the keys and the position beside it carry the same place for everyone else. While the flow's stage or its act's diagram fills a good part of the screen, the navigator's arrows step the hops, its place reads `hop 3 of 33`, and its `j` and `k` leave it, because those letters always leave the flow; past either end the arrows move on. The place follows the screen, so it always says what the next press moves. The bottom control follows a mark at the page's end, because stops lay out as they come near and the page grows while it flies. A phone spans the navigator across the bottom edge above the safe area: a menu that opens the drawer, the arrows as icons, the place, the fold and a check for `reviewed`, each a thumb wide (44 px); the page's top and end move into the drawer, and the ticks, too small to tap, leave. On a phone the header leaves, because claude.ai's own bar above the page already names it: its tools (terms, comment, sound) move into the drawer, above the rail. The rail lists chapters; the open chapter shows its stops, with branch stops folded under the stop that decides them, or its files in the file view.
- **The keyboard works the way native controls do.**
  - *Focus decides what the arrows move.* The flow takes `←`, `→`, `home` and `end` while focus is on the flow or in its bar, its stage, its act's diagram or its dot map; a scene takes them while focus is in the scene. With focus in nothing that takes them, `←` and `→` also step the flow while it fills the screen, as the navigator's arrows do. A code block or the act's diagram wider than its column is a Tab stop of its own, and they scroll it. Anywhere else `←` and `→` step the walk like `j` and `k`, and `home` and `end` go to the page's top and bottom, as the navigator's buttons do. `↑` and `↓` read the page from everywhere but an open sheet, so they also carry the reader on from the flow's section.
  - *The letter keys never change meaning.* `j`, `k`, `[`, `]` and the rest act the same from anywhere, so `j` and `k` always leave the flow or a scene.
  - *A widget is one Tab stop.* The dot map is a tab list whose one Tab stop is the current square. The arrows move among the squares, focus follows the hop, `home` and `end` go to the first and the last, and Tab moves on. A clicked square takes focus the same way.
  - *What the reader cannot see takes no focus.* A closed drawer leaves the Tab order once it slides away, so Tab never lands on a control off screen.
  - *A layer closes the way the reader expects.* A drawer, a sheet and a panel are layers over the page, and [`layers.js`](../walkthrough/runtime/layers.js) closes every one the same way on both kinds of page: `Escape` closes the newest, a press outside it and its opener closes it, and a press on its opener leaves the opener to toggle it. Closing gives focus back to the opener, or to what held it when the layer opened if it names none, unless the reader's pointer already chose a new place. A new layer registers with `WTLayers.open(el, { close, opener })` and calls `WTLayers.closed(el)` when its own control closes it.
  - *Escape hands the arrows back.* It closes the top layer first (a peek, then the newest layer, a selection, comment mode), then moves focus from the flow or a scene to the stop or chapter around it. While the flow holds focus, its keyline says so.
  - *Space and Enter belong to the focused control.* A button presses and a link follows; a square plays the flow. With focus on the page, `space` plays the scene on screen, or the flow on the opening.
  - *A held key repeats a move, never a toggle.* Held, `k` keeps stepping and its cue ticks at one steady pace ([`motion-sound.md`](motion-sound.md#sound)), and an arrow stops at the flow's last hop; `x`, `d`, `m` and the other toggles act once.
- **Links name their target.** Every in-page link previews on hover and focus what it opens and the screen does not show: a stop's gist, and its PR, number and title when the link omits them; a hop's endpoints and story line; a reference entry's title and first sentence; a caveat's compact card; a file's path; a chapter's gist. A link whose row shows all of it has no preview ([`attention.md`](attention.md)). A stop link names its stop's number and title, and its PR outside that PR's chapter, since two PRs each have a stop 07; `render.mjs` refuses one that shows a bare number. Every other cross-reference, such as a caveat card's `where` and `planned` links or a hop's `explained in`, is the same link: a quiet label, then the target's name underlined, peeking on hover and focus, jumping on a click and coming back on `b`. A link to GitHub ends on an arrow out, and one that names a file at the tip carries its lens. Every preview, a term's definition and a hop's peek included, opens when the pointer moves onto its target; a target the page scrolls under a still pointer opens nothing, so a key move never leaves a preview for Escape to close first. A call site's `file:line` link, and each `file:line` receipt in a flag's evidence, shows a lens instead: ten lines of the file at the head of the layer that holds them, the linked line lit, the lines that layer adds marked the way the diff marks them, and the diff's syntax colors. A file's path opens GitHub at the commit that holds its lines: the head, or the base for a file or lines the layer removes. `render.mjs --repo` reads the lines; the link itself opens GitHub, because the page cannot know where the reader's clone lives to open an editor. On a touch screen nothing floats at the finger, which covers what it floats beside: a tap on a term, a badge, a caveat marker or a call site's `file:line` opens what its peek says in a sheet at the screen's bottom, over the navigator, with a link on (the glossary, the caveat's card, GitHub), and lights the tapped word, which the page keeps above the sheet. One sheet shows at a time, and a tap outside, a swipe down, its close button or `Escape` closes it. Text a native `title` alone carries opens in the same sheet at a tap, since a touch screen never shows a `title`. A link to another part of the page follows at the tap, and its back control makes a preview unneeded. A caveat marker on a stop's head or in a hop's footer previews a compact copy of the caveat's card, the same component set closer: the title, what the code does in its first sentence, and the fix with where it is planned, so the peek answers what happens next and not only what is wrong. The touch sheet shows the same card, and a click opens the whole card in the reference. Following one pulses a wash behind what it lands on and leaves a back control at the bottom of the reading column that repeats the question that sent the reader, so they know what to look for.
- **What acts looks like it acts, and nothing else does.** Every element that opens a peek or answers a click shows it at rest, before a pointer finds it, and on a touch screen, which has no hover: a link or a peek target underlines its name (dotted, solid under the pointer or focus), a control is a button with its border, and a row of the act's diagram shows a faint band and its number underlined. A row still ahead of the story, the current row, and anything else whose click does nothing are no targets and wear no such mark. A fact that once sat in a native `title` on plain text, such as a hunk's line range or a frame's file, becomes a peek target: `data-peek`, `tabindex="0"` and the `peekable` underline, so hover, keyboard focus and a tap all reach it. A control's keys peek on hover and on keyboard focus, from its `aria-keyshortcuts` (`keys.js`), never from a native `title`, which shows late and to the mouse alone. The runtime names what it answers in `window.WTTriggers`, and `page-check.mjs` holds both directions on the rendered page: every target looks interactive, and no inert text wears the dotted underline.
- **Every jump comes back.** A link, a peek's link, a rail row, the meter, a glossary hit and `g 0` each push a history entry with the place the reader left: the chapter and view, the stop, the scroll, the stage's hop, and which folds and reviewed stops stood open. The browser's Back, `b` and the back control return to that place whole: folds a landing opened close again, and the folds snap rather than grow, so the scroll lands exact. Forward returns to where Back left. Stepping with the keys and switching views move in place and push nothing. [`walkthrough/runtime/trail.js`](../walkthrough/runtime/trail.js) holds the trail for both kinds of page, and a frame that refuses history writes keeps it in memory, so `b` still returns (`trail.test.mjs`).

## The review loop

- **Walk.** `k` and `j`, or `→` and `←`, step forward and back through stops in wake order, `]` and `[` through chapters. Each stop shows the call stack that led to it as a trail of frames; the frames it adds to the stack the reader just left slide in.
- **Every file.** `d` switches a chapter between its walk and every file, and so do the two view tabs. Both land the tabs under the header, so the view they open starts on screen. The file view lists every changed file with its whole diff, grouped in a reading order the reader can flip (stored data first, or the entry first), and the same keys step through files. Each hunk links to the stop that explains it; each walk hunk links to its whole file. Each test file follows the file it covers as its own section, set in under it and closed until the reader opens it; the file's header, and a walk hunk's, link to it.
- **Mark.** `x` marks a stop reviewed and moves to the next one. A reviewed stop folds to its head and opens again from its `show` control. Rings in the rail and segments in the header fill per chapter; the header shows a segment only for a chapter with stops, since the opening and the close hold nothing to review. A chapter's last mark bursts confetti from the control that made it, and the stack's last mark fires the finale's confetti and plays the whole flow once more, on the close ([`motion-sound.md`](motion-sound.md#celebration)).
- **Start over.** The `?` sheet holds a reset for the folds, which returns diffs and files to their defaults, and one for the review marks, which clears ticks in this browser and in the reader's saved progress, since a later visit would merge a stale copy back. In a PR they act on that PR alone (`reset #5983's folds`, `clear #5983's review marks`); on the opening and the close they act on the whole walk, answered questions included (`reset every fold`, `clear every review mark`). Each asks once, on the button itself: the first press says what it clears (`clear 12 ticks and 3 answers? press again`), and a second press within four seconds acts. The viewer's frame shows no `confirm()`, so a page builds its own.
- **Fold.** Every diff folds from the caret that leads its header, in the walk and in every file, so a reader hides what they have read. This browser remembers each fold, and a reload keeps it. `z` folds or opens every diff of the view: the walk's `fold every diff`, or every file's `open every file`. The navigator carries the same control for the open PR, so it is in reach anywhere in the chapter, and it leaves the opening and the close, which have no diffs. A link to a file opens it; stepping with `j` and `k`, or a reload, only moves.
- **Check understanding.** Each chapter ends with its questions (a checkbox each, linked to the stops that answer it) and its self-check cards.
- **Read the act.** Under the stage, the act's diagram draws the hop's act as a sequence diagram, the notation engineers read without a key: the act's components across the top, a lifeline under each, and one row per hop in story order, an arrow from caller to callee with what travels written above it. A run in passing takes one short gray row, dotted from lifeline to lifeline, with no arrowhead and no number. Heads sit in their system's band, in the outline's order, and a crowded act staggers them over two tiers so every name shows whole in its box. The rows the story has reached keep their arrows and labels, dimmed; the current row is lit, its caller outlined and its callee filled; the rows to come hold their place, empty, so the act builds up and never changes height; a head the story has not reached waits dashed. How the rows move is in [`motion-sound.md`](motion-sound.md#animate-a-process). A click on a row shows its hop and keeps the arrows on the flow, and its peek previews the hop the way a square's does. On a phone the act fits its box with every name at 13 px, so the current hop's caller, callee and arrow all show without scrolling: the heads restack over as many as four tiers, each name wrapping to the columns it gets, a system name too wide for its band staggers with its neighbors, the rows sit closer, the arrows' labels leave the figure, the stage names what travels (`Worker → Hatchet · hatchet · …`), and the key folds under `how to read the arrows`. The dot map below carries the same hops as tabs for a screen reader.
- **Terms.** A marked term shows its definition on hover and focus, and links to the glossary. Inside a link or a button, such as a branch card, a term keeps its hover definition and drops its own link: a control never holds another, because the browser closes the outer one at the inner tag and splits the card and any answer mark in it. `render.mjs` refuses a page that nests one. `t` opens the glossary: every term in reading order, grouped by the PR that first needs it, each with the stop where it first matters, and a filter that takes the reader's first keystroke. Every typed word has to fit some word of a term or its definition, in any order: the whole word, its start, a run inside it, or one slip away (a letter added, dropped, changed or swapped). A term the words name ranks first, and its PR's group leads; clearing the filter restores the reading order ([`walkthrough/runtime/search.js`](../walkthrough/runtime/search.js)).
- **Comment.** `c` turns on comment mode: the next click on any element of the content or the rail places the comment there (a line, a sentence, a label, a diagram's part, a flag, a question, a card, a table row), a dashed ring shows the target under the pointer, and `c` or `Escape` leaves. The header tools and the navigator stay live, so the reader can leave the mode or move. With lines or text selected (click a line number, shift-click another, or drag across the code), `c` comments on the selection at once. A stop's `comment` button comments on the whole stop. See [Comments](#comments).
- **Play the flow.** On the opening chapter, `space` plays the flow one hop at a time in its fixed stage, over the act's diagram, and each run of hops from before the stack as one, in passing. A run in passing reads muted, names the components it passes, and lists its hops under it, open; the stack's hops alone carry numbers (`hop 5 of 41`). The arrows step it while focus is in the flow, and past either end a fresh press moves on, so nobody steps every hop to leave the page. The close holds the same player, so the flow plays in place there, and the hop, the pause and the diagram travel with it. A pause keeps the hop on screen and its clock where it stopped: play runs the rest of the hold with no new render, cue or animation, and a step while paused starts the new hop fresh. The stage shows what a dot's hover shows: the hop, then the stop it wakes, by number, title and gist, so stepping or playing never hides it. In a chapter, `space` plays the scene on screen, and the arrows step it while focus is in it. A step pressed while another draws waits for that one to end, so fast presses all land. `l` turns `listen` on, so play and every step read the flow aloud, when the page has a voice: a recording published with the page, or OpenAI with the reader's own key where the page runs outside claude.ai; `voice ·` appears where the reader can pick or enter a key ([`motion-sound.md`](motion-sound.md#narration)). The key field takes a password input, stays in the reader's browser unless they tick `remember the key on this device`, and is sent only to api.openai.com; the key never reaches the page's data or comments.

## Performance

Speed is part of the review: a reader who waits between stops loses the thread. A review page carries every hunk of every PR, so it keeps in the document only what a reader can reach, and it lays out nothing a frame does not show.

`walkthrough/perf.mjs` is the check. It opens the page fresh for each interaction, traces Chrome's main thread and times each input from the event to the next paint, three runs each, and it fails when a median misses its budget. It finds its targets on any walk, and it skips an interaction the page does not have. Run it where `playwright-core` resolves: `PLAYWRIGHT_CORE=<its entry file> node walkthrough/perf.mjs page.html`. The budgets hold in headless Chrome, whose software raster costs more than a laptop's GPU:

| Interaction | Input to paint | Longest task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Load | first paint in 1.5 s | 150 ms, 900 ms in all |
| A link from the close into a chapter | 100 ms | 70 ms |
| `]` to the next chapter | 80 ms | 50 ms |
| `k` to the next stop | 64 ms | 40 ms |
| `→` to the next hop | 64 ms | 25 ms |
| `→` across acts, with the slide | 64 ms | 33 ms |
| `d` to the every-file view | 100 ms | 60 ms, two frames past 33 ms as its first files arrive |
| Scrolling the longest chapter | | 33 ms, no dropped frame |
| Typing in the glossary | 40 ms | 20 ms |
| `k` to the next part, on a hand-painted page | 64 ms | 40 ms |
| `x` to mark a part | 64 ms | 40 ms |
| `?` to open the keys | 64 ms | 40 ms |

- **Code waits as text.** Code is most of a review page: rendered whole, a ten-PR stack's diff rows are 151,000 of its 181,000 elements. The renderer writes each diff's rows as the text of a `<script type="text/x-lines">` beside an empty block, with what the diff shows at first as `--rows` and `--folds`, and CSS turns those into the block's exact height (a row is 17.25 px, a fold control 23 px), so nothing moves when the rows arrive. The renderer folds the walk's long diffs; the file view folds its own. A stop or a file brings its rows in a screen before it shows. Anything that reads rows brings them in first: a comment's anchor, a selection, the file view's copy of a diff. The open chapter brings in the rest while the reader reads, so find in page reaches every row, and a chapter that closes gives its rows back. The every-file view copies the diffs of its first few open files where it opens, a file's as it nears the screen or a link lands on it, and the rest in idle time. The document holds one chapter's code, and every restyle, layout and query walks that alone.
- **Read, then write, once a frame.** A read of layout after a write lays the page out again on the spot. The page's reads of a frame run together before its writes (`inFrame`). A landing's frame reads one place, the target's, and scrolls: after the chapter's first layout the browser brings the stops at the old scroll position into range, and a second read would lay them out just before the page leaves them. The rail follows a frame later.
- **Answer the press first.** A chapter or view that opens skips its layout in the press's own frame (`content-visibility: hidden`), so that frame paints at once with the old one gone; it lays out in the next frame, and the reads that need it wait with it. Nothing waits before the page's first paint.
- **Focus leaves before a part hides.** Hiding the element that holds focus makes the browser restyle the whole page on the spot to find focus a new home: 60 ms for the stack's close. Focus leaves first, and the part that opens takes it a frame later, once the page is laid out.
- **Find each part where it lives.** A query of the whole document walks every chapter, and one that finds nothing walks all of them. The page finds its one-of-a-kind parts once, in the header, in what floats over the page, in the flow and at the main column's end. It looks for rail rows in the rail, for the glossary's terms in the deck, and for a key's caps in the header, the rail, the navigator, the chapter on screen and what floats over it. A chapter's diffs are indexed once, by the chapter's layer: each PR numbers the diffs of a file on its own, so an id alone can name another PR's diff.
- **Measure near the screen.** A loop that reads layout over a chapter's elements forces the whole chapter's layout in one frame. An `IntersectionObserver` measures each block as it comes near the screen, and the act's diagrams are measured with the first frame's own layout.
- **Measure the few that decide.** The stage holds its tallest hop, so stepping never moves what sits under it. A hop's height follows the length of its words, so the page lays out the dozen longest and every run in passing, whose hops list under it, together, once, and takes the tallest.
- **Restart an animation without layout.** The Web Animations API replays a slide or a frame's entry; reading `offsetWidth` to restart a CSS animation lays out everything pending. A part the page draws anew each time, like a hop on the stage, animates on its own.
- **Idle work in small pieces.** The browser offers an idle callback up to 50 ms, and a key pressed meanwhile waits for the callback to end, so idle work takes at most 8 ms at a time.
- **Skip what is off screen.** Stops, diffs, files and the close's long ledgers off screen skip layout, paint and hit testing (`content-visibility: auto`), each at about the height it will take, so every scroll the page makes follows its target element while the stops above it settle their heights.
- **Land, then settle.** A link that opens another chapter lands on its target as the chapter slides in, then holds it: stops near the landing draw for the first time in the next frames and take their real height (a stop kept at 640 px can draw at 4,000), and scroll anchoring would carry the page that far off the target. Anchoring stays off, and the page lands again each frame until the place holds still for three. A trip longer than two and a half screens starts one screen short, so the spring never renders every stop it passes.
- **Tab stops without layout.** A wide code block must be a Tab stop before the reader nears it. The renderer records each block's longest line, and the page marks a block wide from that and its column's width after the landing frame, erring toward a Tab stop; the measure near the screen corrects it. A block whose rows arrive is measured with the next frame's reads, and the file view estimates a file's blocks in the frame after it copies them.
- **Audit what renders.** An accessibility audit turns skipping off first: axe reads a text's background by hit testing, and a region that skips rendering answers with whatever renders beside it.
- **Measure the page, not the driver.** Time the main thread from a Chrome trace and each input with Event Timing (`PerformanceObserver`, type `event`), and click at coordinates read beforehand: a test driver's own selector engine costs more than the page on a DOM this size.

## Touch

A phone reads the page inside claude.ai's viewer, under the viewer's own bar, with a finger and no keys. A touch screen is one with no hover and a coarse pointer (`(hover: none) and (pointer: coarse)`), whatever its width; a phone is 560 px wide or narrower. Width decides the layout and the pointer decides the keys, so a narrow window under a mouse keeps its keycaps.

- **No keys.** A touch screen shows no keycap, keys sheet or keyline. A control reads the same as under a mouse: its icon or its word, with its name for a screen reader.
- **44 px to land a finger.** Every button, tab, summary, rail row and fold is at least 44 px tall; a caret keeps its look and grows an invisible hit area; a checkbox's row is the target. The flow's squares, an index beside the stage's arrows, are 24 px.
- **Nothing floats at the finger.** A peek opens at a tap in a sheet over the navigator ([Links name their target](#navigation)); text a native `title` alone carries opens there too, since a touch screen never shows a `title`.
- **Fixed bars never cover content.** The main column's bottom padding and `scroll-padding-bottom` hold the navigator's height and `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`; the peek sheet and the drawer stop above the navigator.
- **Nothing overflows the page.** Wide content scrolls inside its own box, which shows a colored right edge while it goes on: diffs, tables, pictures and the domain model, drawn at 1.3× so its labels read at 14 px. The act's diagram fits its box instead ([Read the act](#the-review-loop)).
- **Text at 14 px at least.** On a phone `render.mjs` appends one media block that lifts every smaller pixel size in the runtime CSS to 14 px ([`walkthrough/touch-type.mjs`](../walkthrough/touch-type.mjs)); diagram text keeps the size its layout measures, 13 px for the act's heads. Code lines set `--row` to match.
- **Chrome is not selectable; content is.** A long press on chrome opens no selection or callout, and a drag across a card picks up its words and leaves its controls, as a native app does; a tap flashes no gray, and a press shows through the squash ([`motion-sound.md`](motion-sound.md#juice)). Chrome is every control by being one (a button, a summary, a keycap, a tab, a toolbar, a `nav`, whose rail rows, view tabs and navigator it covers), set once in `page.css`, and the review page's chrome components, set once in `walkthrough.css`: the header and its meter, the navigator, the back and comment pills, the toast, the flow's controls, the stage's head and meta rows (act, `hop N of M`, layer and proof tags, flags), badges, chips and caveat markers with a caveat card's number, every diagram (the act's heads, lanes and row numbers, the domain model), the dot map and its key, the churn bars and counts, a scene's bar and cover, a stop's number and actions, kickers, carets and hints that name a key. Content stays selectable and copies whole: prose (the intent, the brief, takeaways, story lines and subtitles, stop text, reference text, answers), titles, table cells, code and diffs, and the definitions in the glossary and the peek sheet. A new chrome part names its root in `CHROME` ([`components.md`](components.md#selection)) and joins the rule, or `catalog.test.mjs` fails.
- **Every sheet closes by touch.** The glossary sheet carries a close control, and comment mode's hint a `stop` button, since a touch screen has no `Escape`.
- **Check it.** Walk the page top to bottom at 390×844 and 360×740 with touch emulation: no page scrolls sideways, and every component meets the rules above.

## Hand-painted pages

A hand-painted page walks with the review page's keys, words and cues. [`walkthrough/runtime/page.js`](../walkthrough/runtime/page.js) and `page.css` bring them: inline both whole, after `sound.js`, `confetti.js`, `search.js`, `layers.js`, `markup.js`, `keys.js`, `disclose.js` and `trail.js`, the way `render.mjs` inlines its runtime. A single-punch page is exempt.

- **Mark the parts.** `data-page="<key>"` on the element that holds the page names its stored state. `data-part="head"` marks a heading the arrows land on, `data-part="group"` a run of items under one head, usually a `<details>`, and `data-part="item"` what the reader marks reviewed. A `<details>` with an id is a fold this browser remembers. `data-title` names a part in the navigator when its heading runs long.
- **What it brings.** The rules above, with an item for a stop and a group for a chapter: `k`, `j` and the arrows, `↓` through a tall part a screen at a time, `x` to mark and move on with a group's confetti and the page's finale, `z` for the group's folds, the navigator, the `?` sheet with its two resets, `m`, a pulse where a link lands and `b` back from it, and comment mode on `c`.
- **Terms.** `<span data-term="Name">` shows the definition a `wt-glossary` JSON script holds (`[{ "term", "def" }]`), and `t` opens the glossary with its filter. Mark a term where each part of the page first uses it, outside code, links and headings. A page about the code a review page walks takes its terms from that outline, so both pages speak one word list.
- **Caveats.** Write each caveat as the card `page.css` styles, `<article class="caveat" id="cav-<id>">` with its title and its `did`, `why now`, `cost`, `fix` and `planned` rows, and mark each place it names with `<a class="cav-mark" href="#cav-<id>">`: a link with the caveat icon that peeks and lands like any in-page link ([`SKILL.md`](../SKILL.md), Always).
- **Comments.** Declare `{ "comments": { "composer_only": true } }`, and `c` opens claude.ai's composer on the element the reader clicks next. Off claude.ai, `c` says where comments open.
- **What stays with the renderer.** The flow, scenes, the file view, narration and comment pins need a review page's data.
- **Check it.** `PLAYWRIGHT_CORE=<its entry file> node walkthrough/page-check.mjs page.html` presses each key in Chrome at desktop and phone widths, audits what acts against how it looks, prints what changed, and fails a class the catalog lacks ([`components.md`](components.md)); every line reads `ok` or `skip`. `perf.mjs` times the kit's keys on the same page.

## Comments

Comments are claude.ai's own threads: the shell stores them, draws every card and list, and carries a thread to Claude when a writer sends it. The page declares `comments` with `"customAnchors": true` and takes over one job, anchoring, because its chapters and folds hide content the shell's CSS paths cannot follow.

- **Names Claude can read.** Each spot's anchor is plain words, at most 128 bytes: `#5983@9f0fc16 apps/…/command-inbox.repo.ts new L50-57` for lines at a head (the path loses leading folders to fit), `#5983@9f0fc16 <path> whole file`, `#5983 stop 04: Record the delivery before acting`, `#5983 stop 04 flag: interim behavior`, `#5983 stop 04 text: “…”` for a sentence, `#5983 takeaway 2: …`, `#5983 claim 3: …`, `flow hop 5: Publish → Alias pointer` for a square or a row of the act's diagram, numbered among the stack's hops, `flow in passing: WX → Event log` for a run in passing, `flow component: Worker` for a component's head in the act's diagram, `rail: #5983 stop 04: …`. An anchor that names two elements pins on the one that shows. Any other element names its first words in the part that holds it: `#5983 text: “…”`, `opening text: “…”`, `close text: “…”`, `flow text: “…”` for the one flow player wherever it is docked, read with a space at every element edge so a label never glues to its text. Two spots in one part that quote alike add their order: `flow text: “1 · An author publishes a Plan” 2`.
- **Every element is a spot.** The spot under the pointer is the nearest block that holds words. A label, the uppercase first line of a small box like `after`, gives the box it names, and a wrapper that holds the same words gives itself, so one spot stands for one run of words. A diff's header or `@@` line anchors to every line the diff shows, a fold to the lines it hides, and a stop's head and actions to the stop, ringed whole. The ring marks exactly what a click anchors, and the pin comes back to it: each report lists a part's spots once, with the same rule, and finds the quote among them. For a plain comment the platform keeps only the anchor, so the anchor alone must locate the code. The label and the selected code travel with a Send to Claude.
- **Gestures.** The composer opens where the gesture happened, never at the center of a tall element. `c` with a selection calls `compose(anchor, point, { label, detail })` at the selection's end. Without one, `c` turns on the page's comment mode: the page cannot start the shell's mode without opening a composer, so it waits for the next click and composes at that point, which starts the shell's mode. In either mode a drag across code lines comments on the range, and every click in the content or the rail comments, links and buttons included; the header tools and the navigator keep working. `c` or `Escape` leaves: the page's own mode at once, the shell's through `exitMode()`.
- **Pins.** The shell sends the threads; the page reports a point for each one whose spot is on screen: the line, or the fold, closed panel or reviewed stop that hides it. It reports again after a chapter or view change, a fold, a panel, a review mark, a resize, and once a stop that draws for the first time settles. A spot inside a part that skips rendering has no box yet, so its pin stands on the nearest part that has one until that part draws. The rail stays put while the page scrolls, so a pin on it is placed again on every scroll. A report resolves only the chapter on screen; a thread elsewhere has no pin to place. A thread elsewhere stays in the shell's list, and opening it there brings its chapter, unfolds its line and scrolls it to the center. A thread the shell's own click placed (a CSS path) gets a pin when that path still resolves.
- **Absence.** Without `comments` (a local file, a public link) the comment controls hide. Without `customAnchors` the page marks its spots `data-comment-target` and falls back to `openComposer`. A `forbidden` or `unavailable` answer hides them for the visit. The page never retries a refused write and never builds its own thread list.

## Capabilities on claude.ai

Declare these when publishing a review page. A hand-painted page with the kit declares `comments` alone, in its composer-only form ([Hand-painted pages](#hand-painted-pages)). Load the Artifact tool's `artifact-capabilities` skill first: it owns the API and the limits. A declaration is the whole set, so a republish that adds one restates the rest.

| Capability | Config | Feature | Without it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `comments` | `{ "customAnchors": true }` | Native threads anchored to lines, stops, flags, takeaways, claims and hops | Comment controls hide |
| `user` | `{}` | The viewer's id keys their private progress | Progress stays in the browser |
| `db` | `{}` | Private progress that follows the viewer across devices | Progress stays in the browser |

The page writes one document, `data/users/<uid>/progress`: reviewed stops, checked questions and seen chapters, which only that viewer can read. It writes it only when the reader changes something, never on load.

## Privacy

- Progress is private: no one else sees a reader's marks or place.
- Comments are shared with everyone who can comment on the artifact; the shell says so in its own panel. A `comments` declaration keeps the artifact inside the organization.
- The database holds no diff text. The page already carries the evidence, and comment text lives in the shell's store.

## Talking to Claude

- **No chat of its own.** The page declares no `sample` and draws no chat. A chat inside the page answers from only the context the page sends it, so it cannot find a word the reader saw two chapters back. claude.ai's `Chat`, beside the page, reads the whole page. A question about one line or one block goes in a comment sent to Claude, which carries its spot.
- **Send to Claude.** A writer sends a thread from the shell's composer. Claude reads the anchor and the carried code, answers in the thread, and turns an approved thread into a PR review comment only when the human asks for the post.
