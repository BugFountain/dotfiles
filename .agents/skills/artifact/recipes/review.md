# Review

Build one interactive walkthrough of a PR or a stack, for everyone: the author, the reviewers, and teammates who are new to the code. The page traces the change the way control flows, so a reader who walks it end to end understands the whole change set. The parent [`artifact`](../SKILL.md) router owns the theme and delivery; the [walkthrough renderer](../walkthrough/README.md) builds the page from an outline.

## Source

Read the requesting chat before the diff. Use it for intent, rejected options, ownership and constraints. Use the merge-base diff for implementation facts. Use the PR description and its ticket when the chat is not available, and for intent the chat does not state.

For a stack, read [`stacked-prs`](../protocols/stacked-prs.md) before extracting. It owns layer boundaries, look-ahead flags and the claim ledger.

## The walk

The shape follows Plannotator's guided code review: an intent-first opening, then stops in reading order, then questions the reader answers.

**Architecture and data flow first.** A reviewer judges a change by what it does to the system: its components, boundaries and owners, and each datum's life through them. [`extractor-brief.md`](../walkthrough/extractor-brief.md#what-the-walk-shows-architecture-and-data-flow) lists those facts and the field each lands in. The walk shows them; every other hunk waits under `not in the walk`, and every file keeps the whole diff.

- **Opening.** The intent, opening on the problem the change solves, one sentence of behavior before and one after, the brief on a stack page, and three to five takeaways tagged `info`, `important` or `warning` (`warning` only for a behavior shift someone could miss). A takeaway says what changes for a user, an operator or a reviewer, and what to do about it. It defines every term the page has not, and never reports on the PRs themselves: one still open, a defect found along the way without its effect. A term reaches the reader defined: the brief, folded under the before and after lines, defines once the ideas every later section uses, at most ten, one word each, with a link to the hop or stop that shows each, a `skip` line naming the question a reader who can skip it already answers, and a closing line on where the code starts. Each brief item lists the glossary terms it defines, and a term the intro or a takeaway uses without a brief item reads in plain words instead; sections below mark their own terms. The opening's keys shrink to one line pointing at `?`. Then `what changes`, the fastest overview of the change and so the first section after the opening's head: the domain model, open, and the answers to the questions the change raises, folded, under a title the page author picks to name their subject and set beside the model ([Section titles](../WRITING.md#section-titles)). Then the end-to-end map: every hop of the change set, each with its proof level, told as one story. The story spends its words on the stack's hops, and mentions the hops from before the stack in passing: each run of them inside an act plays as one, muted, naming the components it passes, its hops listed under it. The hops sit in acts, the chapters of that story, in the order things happen, and each hop carries one story line that follows from the line before it, so a reader who steps from the first hop to the last understands the stack end to end ([`story-brief.md`](../walkthrough/story-brief.md)). Under each story line the stage shows the hop's subtitle: the code that does it, or the reason it matters. Under the subtitle one footer names each part it holds, so no row reads as a copy of another: `code` (the PR and the stop), `proof`, `unfinished` (a flag and who completes it), `caveat` and `explained in` (the reference entries). A fixed-height stage plays one hop at a time, a run in passing as one, and under it the act diagram draws that hop's act as a sequence diagram: the act's components across the top, a lifeline under each, one row per hop with what travels over its arrow, the current row lit and each new arrow growing from caller to callee ([`kits/motion-sound.md`](../kits/motion-sound.md#animate-a-process)). The stage and the diagram share one screen, and the stage draws no second picture of the hop, so the words and the picture never split ([`kits/attention.md`](../kits/attention.md), A picture sits beside its words). The stage is as tall as its tallest hop at the current width, and the diagram as tall as its act, so stepping inside an act never moves the page. A dot map of every hop, one row per act, follows as the flow's index. An arrow's line says whether the caller waits, moves on, or sets something up before the flow runs, and while the flow plays the act draws itself, one arrow per new hop. The full list waits behind one click and reads as the same story, and, when the page has a voice, `play` reads it aloud while `listen` is on ([`kits/motion-sound.md`](../kits/motion-sound.md#narration)). Last, the layers, bottom of the stack first: each PR's row says why it sits where it does, what it leaves for a later PR and what it finishes for an earlier one, each count opening the flags behind it, and its churn: a bar and the counts of code, tests and generated lines. A large PR's row also says what it holds and whether it splits. A stack's release plan follows the layers, since its rows name PRs the layers have just described: each PR in merge order, what must hold before it merges, what to watch once it deploys, and how to roll it back. A `warning` takeaway links to it. What the tests leave unproven appears only here, as a precondition, and only when CI cannot reach it: a manual check, a staging run, a production signal ([Evidence limits](#evidence-limits)). Then the reference: what a reader looks up while walking, such as the guarantees and who holds each, or how the core format reads, each folded under a title that names its content and linked from the stage of every hop it explains. Plain words stay in the story line; a code shape goes in the reference only when the shape is the answer.
- **Known caveats.** Every non-obvious or knowingly imperfect decision in the change gets a caveat on the page, so a reader never spends time reopening what the authors already know: what the code does, why it stands for now, what it costs meanwhile, the fix, and where the fix is planned. The same caveat lives in the code's docs, a plan's `Known caveats` section or an invariants row, with a one-line pointer in the code beside the decision, and the page's caveat links there. The `known caveats` reference entry holds one card per caveat, a marker on every stop and hop the caveat names opens it where the decision shows, and the release plan links the entry once. An item the page marks open, such as a flag or a claim no layer completes, is a caveat too: it links the caveat that says when and where it closes ([walkthrough README](../walkthrough/README.md#known-caveats)). A decision that is only unusual, not imperfect, still takes a caveat when a reviewer would otherwise stop to question it; a real bug is no caveat, and its stop states it plainly ([Voice](#voice)).
- **One chapter per PR** (a single large PR may take one per flow): gist, intent, before and after, the entry point, takeaways, then two views of the same code: the walk and every file.
- **Stops in wake order.** Each stop is one frame of the call stack waking up: what woke it (`via` and the caller's line, whose lens shows the code around it), the stack of frames above it with the layer that introduced each, the payload in and out, a short detail, and the hunks that decide it. Setup flows come before the runtime flow. The reader steps through stops like a debugger.
- **Stop kinds.** `core` is the heart of the change, `consequence` a caller or downstream update, `support` a helper. A **slow down** marker sits on the one to three stops per chapter where the risk concentrates.
- **Branches.** A fork carries a decision; each branch, including the failure branches (duplicates, races, retries, crashes, kill switches), gets its own stops.
- **Proof chips.** Each test example that reaches a stop shows as a chip: the example name and the branch it covers. A branch no test reaches is listed as untested, in that stop. In every file, each test file follows the file it covers as its own section, closed until the reader asks, and the file's header links to it.
- **Margin.** Migrations, ADRs, docs, types, config and generated files sit beside the stop they serve, not in the flow.
- **Scenes.** A mechanism that happens in time (a race, a retry, a lease, a pointer) gets an animated scene on the stop that explains it ([`kits/scenes.md`](../kits/scenes.md)).
- **Questions.** Each chapter ends with four to eight questions a person answers by reading the code or using the product, and two or three self-check cards. Each question keeps its answer behind `answer`, so the reader tries it first. Open, it quotes the sentences that answer it, each followed by the stop it comes from, by number and title (`01b A missing token fails before the SDK initializes`). The link lands on that sentence, lit, and the way back repeats the question. The heading tells the reader how the list works: `try each one, then open its answer`.
- **Not in the walk.** Each chapter's tabs count the files the walk skips and link to their list after the last stop: each file with one line on what it does and its whole diff, one click away, and `open all` for every one of them. The walk shows the architecture and the data flow; this list and every file keep the rest.
- **Every file.** Beside the walk, each PR lists every changed file with its whole diff, in a reading order (below). Each hunk links to the stop that explains it, and each walk hunk links back to its whole file. The files the walk skips carry `not in the walk` and start closed, and so do tests, generated files, lockfiles and diffs over 400 changed lines; `open every file` opens them all. Every file and every walk diff folds from the caret that leads its header, the reader's browser remembers each fold, and `z` folds or opens every diff of the view. The page carries one copy of each hunk: the view clones the walk's.
- **Glossary.** The chapters' terms merge into one glossary, listed in reading order and grouped by the PR that first needs each term. Each term links to the stop where it first matters, and its peek names that stop. In prose, the first use of a term in each stop or chapter header marks it: a dotted underline, the definition on hover and focus, a link to the glossary. The reader never leaves the stop to learn a word. The glossary's filter takes the words a reader remembers, in any order and with a slip.
- **Reference.** The domain model draws as a chart: every entity a box with its name and at most one line, every relation a labeled arrow, and each piece the stack adds or changes marked `new` or `changed` with its PR, in that PR's color and in words. The entity table folds under the chart. A picture takes the colors of what it holds, and a before and tip pair stands as two panes ([reference entries](../walkthrough/README.md#reference-entries)).
- **Closing.** The flow player again, so the whole flow plays in place once every stop has a tick, then every question, the claim ledger, the look-ahead flags, the history of each file several PRs change, the glossary, and the coverage ledger.
- **One PR.** A page for one PR says each thing once. The opening carries the intent, before, after and takeaways, and the chapter head keeps its title, gist and entry point. The close keeps the flow, the claims, the glossary and the coverage: its chapter already ends on the questions, and the flags and file histories exist only across PRs. Nothing on the page says `layer` or `stack`: the renderer drops the layers list, the layer column and the `[` `]` keys, and says `this PR`. A hop the code made before the PR carries `layer: "base"` and draws gray, so the colors still mean what their legend says.

The page is for walking, marking and commenting: reviewed marks, private progress and native comments follow [`kits/interactivity.md`](../kits/interactivity.md), and the page has no chat of its own. Comments are the one shared layer. Navigation and sound follow [`kits/motion-sound.md`](../kits/motion-sound.md): only the reader's own action moves the current stop or makes a sound.

## Reading order

Start at the edge that costs the most to undo.

- **Stored data first** when the PR changes it (a migration, a schema): stored data, contracts and types, data access, logic, wiring, entry points, UI. A schema outlives the code that writes it, and every layer above it follows from it, so a greenfield CRUD reads from the table up.
- **The entry first** when the PR changes no stored data: the order a request travels, from the endpoint, consumer or handler down. Each lower piece is justified by what its caller needs. A bug fix reads best from its symptom, and a change to an existing flow from where that flow enters.

The walk always runs from the entry, because it follows control. Every file opens in the order the rule picks for that PR (`files.mjs` `defaultOrder`), says why in one sentence, and lets the reader flip it. Tests with no changed subject, docs and generated files come last in either order.

## Disclosure and order

- **Most important first.** Badges, flags and takeaways sort by severity (`warning`, `important`, `info`), source order within a severity. Warning is a solid mark, important an outline, info quiet, so the order reads without color.
- **Progressive disclosure.** A list longer than seven items, or a block taller than a screen, shows its summary first and discloses the rest in place: a count that expands, the first seven items and a `+N more` row, or a fixed-height stage that plays one item at a time. Branch stops fold under the stop that decides them, in the rail too.
- **Close where it opened.** A fold's control opens and closes it, and closing from its end keeps the control under the pointer. Nothing disclosed moves the page on its own.

## Review contract

### Evidence has provenance

Every claim sits beside evidence from the reviewed snapshot. A diagram or a scene explains but does not prove. Environment assembly proves only that pieces are co-located, unless a recorded run shows the behavior.

An outline cites evidence by hunk id (`path#hN`, from `diff-index.mjs`), and the renderer copies the text: nobody retypes a hunk. Each hunk renders verbatim with its `@@` header as `git diff -U3` shows it, with syntax colors that never change a character: the row tint, the sign and the left rule say what changed, and a removed line paired with the added line that replaces it marks the words that changed ([`themes/cyberpunk.md`](../themes/cyberpunk.md#code)). Its label names the path and its lines in words that need no key, `L120–134 at #9489`, or `before #9489` for lines the PR removes, and its tooltip holds the whole meaning: line numbers in the file at that commit, never patch offsets, and the diff copied verbatim. `data-layer` names its layer. The page states no rule about its own evidence in prose: a label or a tooltip says it where the reader meets it. A `focus` range keeps the lines that matter open, with bright line numbers, and folds the rest of the hunk behind a `more lines` control; an excerpt shows only its focus lines. A line over 4,000 characters, such as a compacted snapshot, shows a window around its first difference, with a row that names the characters shown; its file's coverage reads `partial`. Every hunk from an intermediate layer carries `data-base` and `data-head`, so the block names its own layer range instead of the stack's.

### Evidence limits

A page lists a limit of its evidence only when no test in CI can reach it: a manual check, a staging or production run, an outside system the tests cannot fake. Each such limit is a precondition in the release plan, on the PR it gates, with who checks it and how. A gap a test could close is closed before the page ships, never listed: a section such as `what the tests prove, and what they do not` holds only rows a reader acts on, and a row that says `untested` for code a unit test could reach is a gap to fix, not a fact to show. A branch no test reaches still shows as untested in its stop, because that is where a reviewer judges it.

### Claims stay beside deciding code

Show the code that decides each stated behavior, at the stop that states it. Keep a state table next to its predicate hunk, and a user-visible before-and-after next to its source hunk. Quote complete messages, placeholders and line breaks included.

Trace every caller of a changed shared API, hook, query or payload. Each caller wakes as a `consequence` stop, or the stop that changes the API names it in its detail, with what it does with the change (`uses`, `filters`, `renders` or `ignores`) and what it costs (`pays cost`, `cache reuse` or `none`).

Tests support only the branch each example reaches. A proof chip names the example as written and the branch it covers.

Domain-specific evidence follows the same rule:

| Change shape | Show beside the claim |
| --- | --- |
| Optional wire field | Old and new producer and consumer pairings in both deploy orders |
| Retry or durable transition | Key, source of record, expiry, terminal branch, and failure outcome |
| Identity copy or remap | Source id, destination id, persisted reference, and policy result |
| Several user surfaces | Complete source template and consumer for each surface |
| Environment wiring | Assembly evidence, plus separate recorded behavior when it exists |

These are examples, not mandatory sections. Add only what the diff triggers.

### Coverage is measured

Every hunk of every layer has exactly one home: a stop anchor, a proof chip, a margin note, or a file-level housekeeping or everything-else row. `check.mjs` enforces it, so an omission is an error before it is a gap on the page.

The coverage ledger lists every changed path per layer, with its status, its disposition (`primary seam`, `test`, `supporting`, `docs`, `generated`, `housekeeping`, `everything else`), its coverage, its churn and the stop that homes it. Each row carries `data-path`, `data-disposition`, `data-coverage` and `data-churn`, and each layer prints its statement in a `.coverage` element. The ledger stays closed until the reader opens it.

## Voice

- Plain words, short sentences, present tense, active voice. Describe the system as it is, never the diff ("the consumer records the command", not "this PR adds").
- A stop title names the concept, never the file, in 3–7 words the rail shows on two lines. A gist is one sentence a reader can stop at.
- Say each point once. The gist says what the frame does; the detail says only what the gist, the code and the branch cards leave out: why it has this shape, the invariant it keeps, what breaks if it is wrong. A hunk caption appears only when a stop cites several hunks, to tell them apart.
- Code names in backticks, two per sentence at most. Log codes (`#7973989e`) stay in the evidence: beside `#4080` a hash reads as a PR number.
- After verification, an editor pass ([`walkthrough/editor-brief.md`](../walkthrough/editor-brief.md)) tightens the prose without changing a fact.
- Explain; never grade. The page issues no verdict on the change or itself, and the words `Ship`, `Needs gaps`, `Misleading` and `verdict` do not appear in it. A real bug found while reading is stated plainly in its stop, with the line.

## Comments

Comments are claude.ai's own threads, through the `comments` capability ([`kits/interactivity.md`](../kits/interactivity.md#comments)); the page draws none of its own. The page names every spot in words a person and Claude both read, so a thread's anchor finds its code without the page: `#5983@9f0fc16 apps/…/command-inbox.repo.ts new L50-57` for lines at a head, `#5983 stop 04: Record the delivery before acting` for a stop. The page never posts to GitHub. A thread becomes a PR review comment only after the human approves it and asks for the post, against the current head of the PR that owns the line.

## Done

`check.mjs` passes: every hunk homed once, every frame and call site at its line, every reference resolved. Every stop says what woke it and shows the code that decides it. Every changed path appears in the ledger and in its PR's every-file view. Marks sort by severity and long lists disclose progressively. The page scores 10/10 on the [attention audit](../kits/attention.md#audit). The page passes the [Web Interface Guidelines](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/command.md), or names the rule it departs from and why. The page does not grade the reviewed change. Deliver it by [Publish](../SKILL.md#publish) or [Live preview](../SKILL.md#live-preview).
